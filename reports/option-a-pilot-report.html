Option A · Phase 0 · Data-availability research

Is the differentiating data actually out there?

Twenty courses across the six fee-verified universities, checked against what each university publishes on its own course page. Short answer: yes, and there is more of it than the plan assumed — but the traffic case for the pilot six is weaker than the data case.

The data exists and it is richer than expected. Every one of the 20 sampled courses has real, published, course-specific content in at least two of the three categories. Not one course would need to fall back to generic text across the board. The weakest single category is career outcomes, where 2 of 20 publish nothing usable.

But the pilot six are not where the traffic is. Over the last 90 days those six universities' course pages drew 47 impressions and zero clicks — 2% of the site's course-page impressions. That does not argue against Option A; it argues for re-ordering which universities go first. Detail in section 5.

01

What was checked, and how

Twenty courses were sampled across the six universities, stratified by subject (business, computing, engineering, health, law, social science, arts, science) and by level, so the result is not skewed by one discipline's habits. Each course's official university URL was taken from our own data file and fetched live. All twenty returned HTTP 200 with full server-rendered HTML.

Each page was then read — not keyword-matched — for three things: a named module or curriculum list, entry requirements specific to that course beyond an IELTS number, and career outcomes the university itself states. Nothing was inferred, estimated, or filled in. Every grade in the tables below reflects text actually present on the university's page, and the full evidence with per-course notes is committed to reports/option-a-pilot-coverage-2026-09-09.json.

One measurement correction worth stating

Bath's entry-requirement section initially measured at 21,000–37,000 words per course. That is not per-course content — Bath renders an A–Z of every country's qualifications on every course page. The figures in this report count only the course-specific portion (selection criteria, A-level and GCSE requirements, and the Indian-qualification block), which is 200–3,000 words depending on level.

04

What this would actually add to a page

Two of our live course pages were fetched for comparison. Both render roughly 2,250 words in total. At the stated 36.8% uniqueness, that is about 845 genuinely unique words per page, with the remaining ~1,400 shared across every course page on the site.

Adding the mean 1,911 harvestable words would take a page to roughly 4,200 words, of which about 2,750 would be unique to that course — a projected shift from 36.8% to roughly 65%. That is arithmetic on the measured word counts, not a modelled SEO outcome, and it assumes the template surfaces the new content without adding more boilerplate around it.

The more important point is qualitative. A page that states "requires a 2:2 plus significant programming experience, preferably in Java" or "a portfolio may be required if no art-based Higher" is answering a question a student actually has. Our current entry-requirements text says the threshold "varies by department" and invites them to contact us.

05

The finding that should change the plan

The brief asked for phasing based on coverage. Coverage turns out not to be the constraint — availability is high almost everywhere. So the phasing question becomes which universities first, and Search Console has a clear opinion that differs from the fee-verified six.

Read the right way, this supports the initiative rather than undermining it: 96% of course pages earning no impressions at all over a quarter is exactly what thin, near-duplicate content looks like at scale. Option A is aimed at the right problem.

But it means the pilot six were chosen for a reason — they are fee-verified — that has nothing to do with where organic visibility currently exists. The universities actually drawing impressions are a different set:

06

Things found along the way

None of these were the object of the research, and none were fixed — this phase changed no data. They are recorded because two of them affect what a bulk crawl would produce.

  • Blocker · Strathclyde · FIXED

    561 course names were unconverted slugs

    Live page titles read "Advancedcomputerscience", "Accountingfinancedataanalytics", "Bschonscomputersciencesliit". The real names exist only on Strathclyde's own pages, in a two-span H1. All 561 pages were crawled: 541 names recovered and applied; 20 were left untouched because their page now 404s — a withdrawn course is not a licence to invent a title for it. "Psychologyimu" turned out to be "BSc Hons Psychology / Psychology & Counselling (IMU)", which no heuristic could have guessed.

  • Blocker · Auckland · FIXED

    349 course names were scraped metadata blobs

    Names were stored as "Programme name: Bachelor of Commerce Faculty: Business School Type: Bachelors degree". The shape is perfectly regular, so the name was re-extracted rather than guessed, then verified against 25 live pages — 25/25 matched. Faculty and Type were kept as fields rather than thrown away; the crawl phase will want them.

  • Found during the fix · not fixed

    26 course records are unreachable behind duplicate slugs

    22 Strathclyde slugs are shared by 2–3 genuinely different courses — "strath-history" is the BA Hons, the MSc/PgDip and the PhD/MPhil/MRes. Only the first has ever had a page; the rest are invisible, including most research degrees. Durham and Swinburne were already known to have the same bug, so it is at least 35 records site-wide and has never been audited across all 116 files. Fixing it adds URLs rather than changing any, so it needs no redirects.

  • Correction to this report

    Auckland has 278 real programme pages, not 146

    My first draft counted only the blob-prefixed entries and missed 132 programmes whose names were already clean. The accurate split of the 527 is 278 programme pages, 237 subject pages, and 12 subject-area browse pages — the browse pages ("Business and commerce", "Law and justice") are landing pages ingested as courses and are thin by construction.

  • Data accuracy · Auckland

    Published IELTS differs from what we store

    Auckland's BCom page states IELTS 6.0 with no band below 5.5. This is a per-programme published figure, and a crawl of this kind would give us real English requirements as a by-product — the Phase 2 work left open in BUILD-LOG §2 item 12.

  • Housekeeping

    An orphaned Birmingham data file is still in the tree

    data/birmingham-courses.ts holds 112 courses whose every url is the university homepage. The live registry uses bham-courses.ts (603 real URLs), so nothing is broken — but the orphan would poison any script that globs the data directory.

  • Good news

    URL rot is not a problem for the pilot six

    150 URLs probed across the six: 149 alive. A wider probe of 109 URLs across 28 other registry universities came back 85% alive, with three universities fully blocked or dead (West London, Calgary, De Montfort). Expect roughly 15% attrition beyond the pilot.

07

Proposed phasing

The registry is 116 universities and 21,055 courses. Crawling is not the bottleneck — at a polite rate a 600-course university takes under half an hour. The cost is writing and QA-ing a per-university extractor, because every university has its own markup. That cost sorts into three tiers.

Tier A

Server-rendered, one page archetype, structured headings or tables. Bath, Leeds, Birmingham, Strathclyde.

≈ 0.5–1 day each, including QA

Tier B

Multiple page archetypes in one file, or partial coverage needing a fallback path. Auckland.

≈ 1.5 days each

Tier C

Content behind JavaScript or an AJAX form. Edinburgh. Can be descoped to modules-prose plus careers at Tier A cost.

≈ 2–3 days each, or 1 day descoped

PHASE 1

The revised pilot six

Bath · Leeds · Birmingham · Strathclyde · Northumbria · ANU — 3,081 courses · est. 6–8 working days

Edinburgh and Auckland are out; Northumbria and ANU are in. Edinburgh was the clear drop — Tier C, and the worst coverage of the six. Auckland was the second: Tier B, no named-employer careers on any sampled course, and nearly half its file is subject or browse pages rather than programmes. That leaves four Tier A sources with full module and entry coverage, plus two universities that between them carry six times the original six's impressions and need no name cleanup — Northumbria (382 courses) and ANU (344) both have clean names and no duplicate slugs.

The two blockers are already cleared. 541 Strathclyde and 349 Auckland names are repaired, verified and building clean. The Auckland repair was kept even though Auckland left the pilot — it fixes 349 live page titles regardless.

This phase still has to answer a question the research deliberately did not touch: what the template does with the new fields. Some universities publish nothing usable for careers, so every section needs a real skip-on-missing path, the same pattern already used for suppressed fees.

PHASE 2

The universities that already have impressions

~10 universities · est. 8–12 working days

UCL, Toronto, Charles Darwin, UTS Sydney, Surrey, Warwick, UWA, Queensland, King's College London, Edith Cowan — the impression leaders after Northumbria and ANU, which moved into Phase 1. Together with Phase 1 this covers the top 10, about 35% of all course-page impressions.

Run a liveness pre-screen before committing to each — Calgary already failed the probe, and the WAF-blocked list in memory suggests one or two of these will need the curl/TLS workaround rather than Node.

PHASE 3

Re-measure before going further

Decision gate · ~4 weeks after Phase 2 ships

After two phases roughly 14,000 courses are still untouched. Before spending another 4–5 months on the long tail, check whether Phases 1 and 2 moved impressions and average position on the pages they touched. If they did not, the content-uniqueness hypothesis is wrong and the remaining effort is better spent elsewhere.

PHASE 4+

The long tail, only if Phase 3 justifies it

~96 universities · batches of 10 · est. 8–10 days per batch

At that cadence the full registry is roughly 10 more batches, or 4–5 months. My recommendation is not to commit to that up front. Prioritise each batch by impressions rather than file size, and expect ~15% of universities to be unworkable behind bot protection.

Decided: the pilot roster changed

The original six were the right choice for testing availability — they are the universities we know best — but the wrong choice for testing impact, because they carry almost no traffic. Edinburgh and Auckland have been swapped out for Northumbria and ANU, which between them carry six times the original six's combined impressions. Phase 1 now tests both availability and impact at once.

Scope. The research phase touched no code and no data. The follow-up repair changed exactly two data files — data/strath-courses.ts and data/auckland-courses.ts — and only their name field. No page template was touched. Nothing was fabricated: every recovered name came from the university's own page, and the 20 courses whose page has been withdrawn kept their old name rather than receive an invented one. The extraction-effort figures are labelled as estimates throughout.

No URL changed. The course route keys off slug, not name, and a before/after diff confirms the slug and URL sets are identical in both files. Per the URL-stability rule in CLAUDE.md this therefore required no vercel.json redirects — verified rather than assumed. tsc is clean, npm run build is clean, and the rendered out/ HTML was checked to carry the real names in both <title> and <h1>.

Added to the repo: reports/option-a-pilot-coverage-2026-09-09.json (per-course evidence with source URLs and verbatim notes), reports/strath-real-names.json and reports/auckland-name-verification.json (the crawl and verification records), and five scripts under scripts/ — including parse-course-data-file.js, a reusable parser for the three different formats used across data/*-courses.ts. Changes are in the working tree, not committed.

Search Console figures cover 11 June – 8 September 2026. URL liveness probes and all 20 course-page fetches were run on 9 September 2026 and are reproducible from the URLs in the evidence file.
